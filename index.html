Why I'm Drawn to
Heritage Luxury Maisons

I'm drawn to brands that understand what I fundamentally believe: luxury isn't about price—it's about narrative, heritage, and creating emotional connections that last decades.

I've identified five maisons across three luxury ecosystems that exemplify this philosophy. Here's what draws me to each:

LVMH Maisons
Dior Haute Couture

Dior's craftsmanship-first positioning shows that luxury is built from the inside out. Every stitch, every fabric choice, every decision in the atelier communicates exclusivity. The "New Look" legacy isn't just history—it's a blueprint for how heritage brands lead through innovation, not imitation. I want to be part of teams that view production quality not as a cost center, but as the foundation of brand desirability.

Tiffany & Co.

Tiffany's mastery of heritage reinvention fascinates me. They've proven that legacy brands can speak authentically to younger audiences—not by chasing trends, but by deepening the narrative of craftsmanship and timelessness. Their ability to maintain "the Blue Box" as an icon while building Gen Alpha connection is masterful brand strategy.

Prada Group Maisons
Prada

Prada's intellectual approach to luxury is unique. Minimalism as a luxury statement—the belief that precision, restraint, and clarity ARE exclusivity. They've built a maison on the idea that less is earned, not default. This strategic thinking appeals to me deeply.

Miu Miu

Miu Miu proves that a maison can have a distinct voice within a larger group. Playful, confident, unapologetic—Miu Miu shows that luxury can whisper AND shout. Their ability to build a cult following while maintaining brand codes is exceptional.

Richemont Maisons
Cartier

Cartier's mastery of high jewelry storytelling and emotional narrative is unmatched. They understand that jewelry isn't an accessory—it's a moment, a memory, a relationship. Their clienteling philosophy and community-building approach to luxury is masterclass in customer psychology.

Chaumet

Chaumet's heritage (founded 1780) combined with their modern positioning shows how to honor savoir-faire while staying culturally relevant. They're a maison that understands legacy doesn't mean stagnation.

Independent Luxury
Graff

Graff's boldness fascinates me. In a luxury world that often whispers, Graff is unapologetic about maximalism and power. They've built a maison on the belief that luxury can be confident, statement-making, and still refined. This is strategic positioning at its finest.

What Unites These Maisons

All of them share:

— Craftsmanship-first positioning (not trend-chasing)
— Distinct visual & narrative codes (not generic luxury speak)
— Emotional connection building (not transactional selling)
— Heritage respected, but not reverenced (innovation within codes)
— Deep understanding of their specific customer psychology

My Contribution

Emerging Market Expertise: I have on-the-ground insight into how Asian luxury consumers—particularly Vietnamese and multicultural audiences—interact with heritage brands. I can help each maison identify growth opportunities in emerging markets authentically, without diluting codes.

Data-Informed Strategy: I see brand positioning, experiential marketing, and analytics as integrated discipline. Use data to understand emotional drivers → create experiences that deepen connection → measure lifetime value and brand sentiment. This is how modern heritage brands grow.

Authentic Luxury Mindset: I don't treat luxury as a positioning exercise. I believe in these codes. A luxury customer evaluates whether your brand is "worth my capital and time" at every touchpoint. I'm committed to winning that evaluation.

"I want to spend my career at maisons that don't just sell beautiful things—that build communities of people who believe in the code."

Tiffany & Co.
Heritage Experience
Overview

Project Type: Brand repositioning framework (academic brand exploration).

Strategy: Designed a framework to position Tiffany & Co. as a contemporary gallery experience for high-net-worth collectors.

Key Insight

Rather than compete on price or novelty, position Tiffany's retail locations as curator spaces—where collectors gather to appreciate craftsmanship, understand heritage, and build relationships with the brand. This transforms transactional retail into community.

Framework Components

Personalized Engagement: High-touch relationship building with high-net-worth collectors through exclusive previews and heritage narratives.

Experiential Differentiation: In-store experiences (masterclasses, heritage talks, curator tours) that deepen emotional connection to brand and product.

Long-term Loyalty: Strategy focuses on lifetime customer value, not transaction volume.

"Legacy brands don't die because they're old. They die because they forget why they were loved in the first place. Tiffany's job isn't to become trendy—it's to remind collectors why timelessness matters."
Relevance to LVMH

This demonstrates strategic thinking around: (1) How heritage brands evolve without losing identity, (2) Collector psychology and high-net-worth customer behavior, (3) Experiential differentiation in retail, (4) Long-term loyalty strategy applicable to Dior, Givenchy, and Celine globally.

Burberry
Sustainability Positioning
Overview

Project Type: Brand positioning framework.

Challenge: How to position sustainability in luxury without appearing to sacrifice exclusivity or quality.

Strategic Positioning

Core Argument: Sustainability is not a compromise on luxury—it is an extension of craftsmanship, exclusivity, and heritage preservation.

Ethical Sourcing as Premium Innovation: Frame responsible material sourcing not as "doing the right thing," but as "ensuring the longevity and quality of what we make."

Heritage Preservation: Connect sustainability to heritage: "We are stewards of the materials, practices, and cultures that make our products possible. Protecting them protects our legacy."

"Luxury customers aren't idealistic—they're pragmatic about quality. When you position sustainability as 'ensuring your investment lasts,' you're speaking their language."
Relevance to LVMH

This demonstrates understanding of: (1) How to integrate sustainability into luxury brand narratives without diluting exclusivity, (2) The psychology of high-net-worth consumers around environmental concerns, (3) How heritage brands can lead on sustainability as a natural extension of their values—increasingly critical for LVMH's global strategy with younger luxury consumers.
